Notes
  • Returns & dollars: All values are in today's dollars. Returns are real (inflation-adjusted) — inflation is assumed to be zero because both portfolio values and spending are expressed in constant purchasing power. Defaults to 3% (configurable) pre-retirement and 2% (configurable) post-retirement.
  • Pre-retirement taxes: Annual savings contributions are post-tax and deposited at their full stated amount. Portfolio gains during pre-retirement are subject to a 25% (configurable) effective tax rate, reducing the net return on the growing portfolio. Set this rate as a weighted average across your accounts — gains in tax-advantaged accounts (401k, IRA) are not taxed annually while gains in taxable brokerage accounts are; calibrate accordingly.
  • Taxes in retirement: A 15% (configurable) effective rate is applied to all retirement income — both Social Security and portfolio withdrawals. This blended rate should reflect your mix of fully taxable income (traditional IRA/401k draws), partially taxable income (Social Security), and tax-free income (Roth). Annual spending in retirement is your desired net after-tax amount; the model grosses up withdrawals accordingly so that amount is actually achieved.
  • Social Security: $60,000/year (configurable) combined benefit in today's dollars, beginning at average age 70 (configurable). Income is modeled as a single combined cash flow starting when Jon and Grace's average age reaches the configured start age — a simplification that introduces a timing approximation of roughly 1–2 years relative to modeling each spouse separately. After SS starts, after-tax SS income reduces the required portfolio withdrawal for the year.
  • Cash flow timing: Half-year convention — savings deposits and retirement withdrawals are assumed to occur mid-year, earning half a year of investment returns. This produces more accurate results than assuming all flows happen at year-start or year-end.
