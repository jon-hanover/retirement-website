Key Assumptions
  • Returns & dollars: All values are in today's dollars. The annual return slider is a real (inflation-adjusted) return — inflation is assumed to be zero in the model because both portfolio values and spending figures are expressed in constant purchasing power.
  • Income taxes (pre-retirement): —. Conservative all-in effective rates (federal + ~5% state avg + FICA) by bracket: ≤$50K → 20%, $50–100K → 26%, $100–200K → 32%, $200–400K → 37%, $400–600K → 40%, >$600K → 43%. Savings rate is applied to after-tax income.
  • Taxes in retirement: An 18% effective rate is applied to taxable retirement income — conservative for a high-wealth couple but lower than working-year rates due to reduced income. 85% of Social Security benefits are treated as taxable (the IRS threshold applies to anyone with combined income above $44K/year, which this couple exceeds). 55% of portfolio withdrawals are treated as taxable, reflecting a realistic mix of traditional IRA/401k (fully taxable ordinary income), taxable brokerage accounts (long-term capital gains), and Roth accounts (tax-free). The retirement spending input is your desired net after-tax spending; the model grosses up portfolio withdrawals accordingly so that amount is actually achieved.
  • Social Security: $40,000/year combined benefit in today's dollars, beginning at average age 67 (year 2055) — the full retirement age for Jon (b. 1986) and Grace (b. 1989). No SS income assumed before age 67 regardless of retirement date. After SS starts, after-tax SS income reduces the required portfolio withdrawal for the year.
  • Cash flow timing: Half-year convention — savings deposits and retirement withdrawals are assumed to occur mid-year, earning half a year of investment returns. This produces more accurate results than assuming all flows happen at year-start or year-end.
